## Why a lesson of its own

A group of Katakana letters look much alike, and this is where learners stumble most. The rules below help you tell them apart by **stroke direction**.

## Two famous pairs

| Pair | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **シ** (shi) and **ツ** (tsu) | Both have two short strokes and a long one. In **シ** the two short strokes are **stacked** on the left and the long stroke sweeps **up** to the right. In **ツ** the two short strokes sit **side by side** on top and the long stroke is pulled **down**. |
| **ソ** (so) and **ン** (n) | **ソ**: short stroke on top, long stroke pulled **down**. **ン**: short stroke on top, long stroke sweeping **up** to the right. |

A memory aid: **シ and ン** have a long stroke that **flicks up**; **ツ and ソ** have a long stroke that **goes down**.

## The other pairs

- **ノ** and **メ**: ノ has one stroke; メ is two crossing diagonals.
- **ク** and **ワ**: ワ has a flat roof on top, ク starts with a slanted stroke.
- **ナ** and **メ**: ナ has a horizontal bar, メ does not.
- **ヌ** and **ス**: ヌ has a crossing diagonal, ス has just two strokes.
- **コ** and **ユ**: ユ has an extra long bar underneath.
- **チ** and **テ**: チ starts with a slanting stroke; テ has two bars.
- **ウ** and **フ**: ウ has two short strokes on top.

## Practice

Use the flashcards to review these letters again and again. There are no new letters here; the goal is to see them and read them correctly at once.
