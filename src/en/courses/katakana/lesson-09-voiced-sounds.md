## Two familiar marks

Katakana uses exactly the same two marks as Hiragana:

- **゛** (two small strokes) makes a **voiced** sound: k becomes g, s becomes z, t becomes d, h becomes b.
- **゜** (a small circle) makes a **semi-voiced** sound, only with the ハ row: h becomes p.

| Base letters | With ゛ | With ゜ |
|---|---|---|
| カ キ ク ケ コ | ガ ギ グ ゲ ゴ | |
| サ シ ス セ ソ | ザ ジ ズ ゼ ゾ | |
| タ テ ト | ダ デ ド | |
| ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ | バ ビ ブ ベ ボ | パ ピ プ ペ ポ |

**ジ** is read **ji**. **チ** has a voiced form **ヂ** and **ツ** has **ヅ**, but they sound like ジ and ズ and are very rare, so this course does not teach them separately.

## Loanwords

パン (bread) comes from Portuguese, not English. ペン, バス, ドア, ダンス and ガス are close to English. **バナナ** has 3 beats: ba, na, na. **ズボン** (trousers) comes from French.
