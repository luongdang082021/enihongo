## Three scripts

Japanese uses three scripts at the same time:

- **Hiragana** (ひらがな): the soft, rounded script, used for native Japanese words, particles and word endings. It has about 46 basic letters.
- **Katakana** (カタカナ): the more angular script, used for loanwords and foreign names.
- **Kanji** (漢字): Chinese characters, used for the core meaning of many words. You learn them gradually, later on.

Hiragana comes first because any word can be written in Hiragana. Japanese children's books are written entirely in Hiragana.

## The mora (beat)

Each Hiragana letter is usually **one beat** (a mora), an evenly timed unit of sound. For example さくら (sakura) has 3 beats: sa, ku, ra.

A few special cases come later: the small ゃ ゅ ょ join the letter before them to make one beat, while the small っ and ん each take a beat of their own.

## How to study this course

1. Learn only 5 to 10 letters per lesson. Do not try to finish everything in one sitting.
2. Look at the letter, say it out loud, and only then flip the card to check.
3. Do the mini quiz at the end of the lesson. Getting 7 out of 10 correct completes the lesson.
4. The next day, reopen the previous lesson and redo its quiz before starting a new one.

Japanese in this course comes with romaji (the reading in Latin letters) so you can check yourself. Drop the romaji as soon as you feel comfortable.
