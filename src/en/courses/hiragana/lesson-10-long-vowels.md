## The rule

A long vowel is a vowel held longer, and it takes **one extra beat**. In Hiragana, you add a vowel letter after the letter that has that vowel:

| Sound | How it is written | Example |
|---|---|---|
| aa | add **あ** after a letter from the あ, か, さ... rows | おかあさん (okaasan) |
| ii | add **い** after a letter with "i" | おにいさん (oniisan) |
| uu | add **う** after a letter with "u" | くうき (kuuki) |
| ee | usually add **え** | おねえさん (oneesan) |
| ei | add **い** after a letter with "e" | せんせい (sensei) |
| oo | usually add **お** | おおきい (ookii), とおい (tooi) |
| ou | add **う** after a letter with "o" | おとうさん (otousan), ありがとう (arigatou) |

The last two are easy to mix up: "ei" is always written えい, and "ou" is always written おう (not ええ or おお). When you type romaji in this course, follow the Hiragana spelling: ei, ou.

## Short or long changes the meaning

- **おばさん** (obasan) means aunt. **おばあさん** (obaasan) means grandmother.
- **おじさん** (ojisan) means uncle. **おじいさん** (ojiisan) means grandfather.

Whether you say it short or long decides who you are talking about. When you read, hold the vowel for a full two beats.

## Reading practice

Count the beats as you read: おかあさん has 5 beats (o, ka, a, sa, n), and せんせい has 4 beats (se, n, se, i).
